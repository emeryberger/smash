## Reading

LARGE_ONLY mode in its current form is a net loss on this workload. Peak
RSS increases by ~5 % and wall-clock overhead is ~25 %.

Why peak goes up:
- Smash reserves a contiguous virtual region that backs its compressed-arena
  spans. Pages get committed as the workload allocates large buffers; the
  reservation is on top of system-malloc pages still in use elsewhere.
- The stuff that actually goes through smash on this workload (allocations
  ≥ 16 KiB) is mostly hot — model weights, activation tensors that the
  compiler is currently rewriting. Hot pages don't reach the cold timeout,
  so smash carries the per-page tracking + arena overhead with no
  compression benefit.

Why wall goes up:
- mprotect/soft-dirty ticks every second on a multi-GB committed range
  cost real CPU even with soft-dirty's pagemap read.
- Per-allocation routing through smash's call-site arena hash + slab
  bookkeeping is non-zero overhead (alloc8 strong-aliasing is fast but
  not free).

The conclusion the data forces: **LARGE_ONLY at the current 16 KiB
threshold doesn't catch the compressible mass on this workload.** The
compressible mass is the small-object slab — Tensorizer IR nodes, graph
metadata, intermediate Python objects — most of which are < 16 KiB.

## Options

1. **Lower the threshold**: try `SMASH_LARGE_ONLY_THRESHOLD=4096` or
   `=1024`. Brings more of the slab into smash without requiring full
   mode. Doesn't get the per-call-site arena routing because LARGE_ONLY
   uses the simple `LargeAlloc` path.
2. **Make full mode work**: the user's preferred path because it gets the
   per-(call-site, size-class) arena routing and dictionary training that
   the per-page-LZ4 LARGE_ONLY path can't. Blocked on the
   ProcessPoolExecutor child crash, which (per `bup2h2wu9` 2026-06-01)
   reproduces even with `SMASH_NO_COMPRESSOR=1` — so it's an allocator-
   path bug, not a compressor bug.
3. **Skip neuron-cc on this HLO**: this workload may be fundamentally
   bad for compression (working-set is mostly hot, dataset fits in RAM,
   compile is < 10 minutes). Smash's design point is long-running
   processes with substantial cold heap; one-shot compiles aren't it.
