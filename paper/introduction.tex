\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A significant fraction of allocated memory is cold at any given
moment.  Studies of production workloads at Google and Microsoft
report that 20--50\% of resident pages have not been accessed in the
last 60 seconds~\cite{maas2020adaptive,amaro2023memory}.  This cold
memory cannot be reclaimed without swapping, which incurs
millisecond-scale I/O latency.

\sys is a drop-in memory allocator that transparently compresses cold
heap pages to reduce RSS\@.  It interposes on \texttt{malloc} and
\texttt{free} via library interposition~\cite{berger2001composing},
requiring no application changes.  A background thread periodically
compresses pages that have not been accessed recently; a signal
handler decompresses them on demand.

The design incorporates three techniques.  First, the compressor
zeros freed object slots before compressing each page, replacing stale
data with compressible zero runs; zeroing is deferred to compression
time using non-temporal stores, keeping \texttt{free()} on its fast
path (\S\ref{sec:design:zero}).  Second, \sys hashes the return
address of each allocation to route it to one of four arenas, so that
allocations from the same call site land on the same pages, producing
structurally homogeneous page content (\S\ref{sec:design:arenas}).
Third, \sys applies \emph{JIT compression}: like a JIT compiler that
applies increasing optimization to hot code, \sys applies increasing
compression to cold data---recently cold pages are compressed with LZ4
($\sim$1\,GB/s); pages that remain cold for 60 seconds are
recompressed with zstd-9 for better ratios; accessed pages are
decompressed and reset, analogous to deoptimization
(\S\ref{sec:design:adaptive}).

We evaluate \sys on five applications spanning in-memory databases,
caches, and analytics engines.  \sys reduces RSS by 17--80\%
depending on the workload's hot/cold partition.  Throughput impact is
within measurement noise for most benchmarks.  An ablation study
across 17 configurations quantifies each technique's contribution.

To understand how much of the benefit comes from the compression
pipeline versus the allocator design, we build a compress-only
variant that runs \sys's pipeline atop the unmodified system
allocator.  This variant achieves 32--61\% RSS reduction on its own.  The
pipeline and allocator design are synergistic: \sys's allocator further reduces RSS by 10--25\% on workloads
dominated by large allocations, where freelist pollution and object
mixing span entire pages, while the pipeline's overhead is lower
when integrated into the allocator.

This paper makes two contributions:

\begin{itemize}
\item The design and implementation of \sys, a drop-in allocator that
  transparently compresses cold heap pages using allocator-level
  structural knowledge, including deferred zero-on-free, call-site
  arena segregation, and JIT compression (\S\ref{sec:design}--\ref{sec:impl}).

\item An evaluation decomposing the sources of RSS reduction: the
  compression pipeline, the allocator design, and individual
  techniques, across five applications and nine allocator substrates
  (\S\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{itemize}
